Almost every interface I use is silent, and the few that are not usually make
me want to turn the volume off. That is a strange outcome for a material with
this much potential.

The problem is not sound. It is that most UI sound is decorative — added at
the end, applied everywhere, tuned to be noticed. Once a sound is asking for
attention, it is competing with the thing it was supposed to support.

## Confirmation, not decoration

The sounds that work do one job: they confirm something the interface already
told you visually. A toggle that clicks is not adding information. It is
agreeing with you.

That framing kills most candidates immediately. Scroll does not need a sound.
Hovering does not need a sound. Loading almost never needs a sound. What
benefits is the small set of moments where you committed to something and want
to know it landed.

## Restraint is the whole technique

On this site there are exactly two. Switching the season on the background
plays a toggle. Opening a project plays a soft press. That is it.

I built more than two and removed the rest, which is the part I would repeat.
Every sound I cut made the two that remain feel more intentional. A sound that
fires constantly becomes noise no matter how well designed it is.

## Why synthesis matters

These are generated live with the Web Audio API rather than loaded as audio
files — no network request, no format negotiation, a few kilobytes total. That
is not just a performance nicety. It means the sound can respond to state
instead of being a fixed recording, which is the part I want to explore next.

Sound is not a finishing touch. It is a channel most interfaces leave
completely empty.
